Where to Fish Near Greenville, SC

Taylor LeBlanc · September 12, 2026 · largemouth, smallmouth, Bartram's bass, spotted bass, trout, panfish

Most of the questions I get are some version of where to fish near Greenville. Here is the public water, sorted by how far it is from downtown, with what swims in it and how you reach the bank. Every place on this page is listed by a public agency and open to anyone with a license. My own spots, the small lakes where the biggest largemouth live, are not here, and the last section says why.

Inside 20 minutes

The Reedy River, downtown. The river runs through the middle of the city and the Swamp Rabbit Trail runs along it, with public access all the way to Simpsonville. It holds redbreast sunfish and largemouth, including a few bass bigger than a downtown river has any business holding. After a hard rain it turns the color of clay and takes about three days to clear. I wrote it up in full: the Reedy River in downtown Greenville.

Oak Grove Lake Park. A county park on the east side with an 18-acre lake and a paved loop around it, so the bank is open most of the way round. Greenville County Rec stocks it with bass, bream and catfish and asks for catch and release. It is free, and it is where I would take a kid for a first fish. Oak Grove Lake, Greenville Rec.

Paris Mountain State Park. Lake Placid sits just inside the gate, about ten minutes north of downtown, with bass, sunfish and catfish and a self-serve kayak kiosk at the water. Park admission applies. Paris Mountain State Park.

Inside 30 minutes

The Saluda River. The main river west of town, bank and wade, with largemouth, spotted bass and panfish. The access improved this year: Piedmont Riverfront Park opened in May 2026 and, with Dolly Cooper Park in Powdersville, bookends the first nine miles of the Saluda River Blueway, so there is parking and a put-in at both ends.

The Saluda forks, for trout. The South Saluda and North Saluda have hatchery-supported water that SCDNR stocks, and the Middle Saluda inside Jones Gap State Park was the first river protected under the state's scenic rivers program. It holds wild trout, and from the lower footbridge down to Hugh Smith Road it is catch and release all year, single-hook lures and flies only. SCDNR's Trout Book maps the access: the SCDNR Trout Book.

The foothill creeks. North of town the creeks are wade water for Bartram's bass, smallmouth, spotted bass and largemouth. I name the region and stop there. The SCDNR access map at the bottom of this page shows the public pull-offs.

Pleasant Ridge County Park. Off Highway 11 near Marietta, a four-acre lake with bank and pier access and bass, sunfish, catfish and stocked trout in season. No boats. Pleasant Ridge Park, Greenville Rec.

Lake Robinson and Lake Cunningham. Greer's two drinking-water lakes, run by Greer CPW and open to fishing. Robinson is about 800 acres with a ramp, three fishing piers and bank access in the park between the ramp and the dam; largemouth are the main fish, with catfish, sunfish and crappie. Cunningham is the smaller one at about 200 acres. Motors are limited and you buy an access pass at the warden's office before you fish. Greer CPW lakes.

About an hour

The big reservoirs. Hartwell, Keowee and Jocassee are the boat water, and all three have public bank access through parks: Devils Fork State Park on Jocassee, Keowee-Toxaway State Park on Keowee, and the Corps of Engineers parks around Hartwell. Jocassee holds the state records for smallmouth, spotted and redeye bass. Hartwell hosts the Bassmaster Classic again in March 2027.

The delayed harvest streams. The Chattooga, Chauga, Eastatoee and Cheohee have sections SCDNR stocks heavily and manages as catch and release with single-hook artificials from 1 November to 14 May. From 15 May the normal creel applies. The Trout Book has the boundaries.

Western North Carolina. The French Broad and the other WNC rivers are where I go for smallmouth in fall, about an hour north. You need a North Carolina license there.

Bank, wade or kayak

From the bank: the Reedy, Oak Grove, Lake Placid, Pleasant Ridge, the park at Robinson, and the reservoir parks. Wading: the Saluda and its forks, the foothill creeks, the delayed harvest streams and the WNC rivers, wet in summer and in waders the rest of the year. By kayak: Robinson and Cunningham, Lake Placid, the Saluda between the two Blueway parks, and the reservoirs.

Licenses and rules

South Carolina requires a freshwater fishing license from age 16, and you can buy one online in a few minutes: SCDNR licensing. North Carolina needs its own. The delayed harvest and Jones Gap sections carry their own rules, and the parks keep their own hours and fees. For everything else, SCDNR's public fishing access map lists bank and pier sites, ramps and what swims where, county by county.

What this page leaves out

The small, clear lakes within 45 minutes of town where the biggest largemouth live. I fish them on foot, on lunch breaks, and it took years to find them. The exact spot is the one thing I keep to myself, and I ask the people who fish with me to do the same. That is what keeps those fish where they are. What I can hand you is the method: the seasons page says where the fish sit month by month on any small lake, and a post on reading a lake from a map before you walk it is in the queue. If you would rather skip the years, the trips page has what runs when.
